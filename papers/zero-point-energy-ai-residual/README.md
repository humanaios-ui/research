# Zero-Point Energy and Residual Behavioral Divergence in AI Systems

**Status:** Draft research note  
**Slug:** `zero-point-energy-ai-residual`  
**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Context:** Exploratory mapping between quantum vacuum / zero-point energy concepts and measurement of residual behavioral divergence in AI systems (ACAT / HumanAIOS).

## Abstract

Zero-point energy is the irreducible ground-state energy of a quantum system. Even at absolute zero, the Heisenberg uncertainty principle forbids a perfectly still state. In quantum field theory the same principle produces vacuum fluctuations whose density is theoretically enormous, yet the observed cosmological constant is tiny — the cosmological constant problem.

This note explores a structural analogy between that physics and the measurement problem addressed by ACAT: AI systems retain residual divergence between self-reported disposition and demonstrated behavior even after calibration, fine-tuning, or strong constraint. The residual is not merely noise that more scale will eliminate; it is structural. Boundary conditions (prompts, protocols, multi-agent topologies) act like Casimir plates, selectively suppressing or amplifying behavioral modes. Radiative instability has an analogue in the amplification of small residuals under further training, deployment shift, or recursive loops.

The mapping does not claim that AI systems harvest zero-point energy. It supplies a precise language for treating residual divergence as an intrinsic feature of high-dimensional systems operating under pressure, and for designing measurement and governance regimes that quantify and stabilize that residual rather than assuming it can be driven to zero.
